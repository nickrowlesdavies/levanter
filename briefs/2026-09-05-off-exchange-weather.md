## What this is

The second Off-Exchange, teased at the foot of issue 1: "There is a real market in
temperature, it has real hedgers, and almost nobody outside the utilities knows it is
there." It rides inside the last Signal of the month, same as issue 1. Target is the late
October 2026 Signal, but the date on the file is simply the day it is published.

## The angle, and why weather is the right second subject

Issue 1 was a market that cannot exist. A cask resists every requirement an exchange has:
it is not fungible, has no deliverable grade, has no carry curve, and does not trade
continuously. The absence of the market was the information.

Weather is the mirror image, and that is why it belongs second. Temperature is not
physical, not deliverable, and not something you can hold, and yet a real, cleared,
exchange-traded market exists on it. The piece should turn on how that was possible when
whisky was not. The answer is standardisation by index: you cannot standardise a barrel,
but you can standardise a number. Reduce temperature to a published index, cash-settle
against it, and the four requirements are suddenly met. So the through-line of the column
holds: exchanges list what they can standardise, clear and close out. Whisky could not be
reduced to a number without destroying it. Weather could.

The second half of the hook is obscurity. This market exists, clears, has genuine hedgers
behind it, and almost no retail investor knows it is there, which is the exact inverse of
the cask trade, where retail is being sold something with no market behind it at all. That
contrast is the piece: one market loudly sold and absent, one quietly real and unknown.

## What to nail down, and treat every figure as unverified until sourced

These are leads, not facts. Nothing goes in the copy until it traces to a named source with
a URL and a verification date in the harness corpus, the same gate issue 1 passed. Given how
much the weather product line has changed over the years, currency matters more here than
usual: check the state of the market as of the writing date, not as of some article from
five years ago.

- The mechanic. Heating Degree Days and Cooling Degree Days, measured against a reference
  temperature, aggregated over a month or season, cash-settled against a published index for
  a named city. Get the reference temperature, the accumulation window and the settlement
  source exactly right, and explain them in plain words.
- The history. When the over-the-counter market began, when the exchange first listed
  weather contracts, and the arc since: the contraction in listed contracts around the start
  of the decade, and the more recent expansion in cities and demand. State the current
  position, not a snapshot from an old piece.
- The hedgers. Who actually holds these and why: energy and utilities first, then the longer
  tail of agriculture, construction, retail, events, leisure. A concrete hedge told simply,
  a utility protecting a warm winter's lost demand, earns its place more than a list.
- The scale. Any notional, open-interest or volume figure must be current and attributed. If
  a reliable current figure cannot be found, say the market is small and specialised rather
  than reach for a stale number.
- The limits. This is not a free lunch. Basis risk between the index city and the hedger's
  own location, thin liquidity outside the core contracts, and the modelling problem that a
  changing climate makes the historical distribution a shakier guide than it looks. The
  honest close should sit here, in keeping with the brand.

## The point to land

Same shape as issue 1, opposite conclusion. The presence of this market is also information:
it shows what it takes to make the intangible tradable, which is a standardised, published,
cash-settled index and a clearing house behind it. Whisky failed that test and weather
passed it, and the reason is the whole lesson. No recommendation, no direction call, no
"you should trade this". It is a piece about why the market exists, not an invitation to use
it.

## The one operational step that is not optional

`reports/` is gitignored, and this copy is hand-written, not generated by any build step.
The CI persist force-add only re-commits files that already exist on the runner after the
build, so a hand-written file in `reports/` never reaches CI on its own. This is exactly why
issue 1's first deploy logged "build_offexchange: no issues found" and shipped nothing.

So once the markdown and its docx exist, they must be force-added and committed:

    git add -f reports/substack/levanter-offexchange-weather-<date>.md \
               reports/substack/docx/levanter-offexchange-weather-<date>.docx

Then the next deploy builds `/off-exchange/<date>/`, updates the index, and registers both
in the sitemap. Do not assume the persist step will pick the file up. It will not.

## What is already done, so do not rebuild it

`build_offexchange.py` reads `reports/substack/levanter-offexchange-*.md`, renders the page
and index, and merges into the one sitemap. It calls no `source_guard`, so no market feed
can block it. The aside framing and the Off-Exchange disclaimer are added by the page type,
so the copy does not need to restate the scope caveat. Issue 2 will appear at
`/off-exchange/<date>/`, will be numbered No. 2 from its series line, and will slot into the
index and the prev/next chain automatically. Nothing in the generator needs touching.

## Naming and structure

`reports/substack/levanter-offexchange-weather-<date>.md`, docx beside it in `docx/`,
matching the channel convention. Mirror issue 1's structure so the generator parses it
cleanly:

- An `# H1` title as the first line.
- The series line as the second content line, italic:
  `*Off-Exchange · No. 2 · a monthly series on markets that barely trade, or do not exist at all*`.
  The generator reads the number from here.
- A single bold standfirst paragraph next. This becomes the meta description and the index
  standfirst, so make it carry the piece on its own.
- `## H2` section headings, `---` where a rule helps, and a short italic sign-off. If a third
  subject is chosen, tease it in the closing line the way whisky teased weather; if not,
  leave the tease out rather than promise something undecided.

## The gates, unchanged from issue 1

Three, in order. Every figure, date, proper name and quotation traces to a named source in
the corpus. A mechanical scan for the banned constructions and the em-dash cap. Then the
prose measured against Nick's published baseline, all six measures in band. The harness lives
outside this repo at `Documents/Claude/Claude Outputs/Levanter Off-Exchange`; reuse it, and
add the weather sources to the corpus with their URLs and verification dates.

## What not to do

No direction call and no scored claim, the same as everything under Off-Exchange. Keep it off
the dashboard and away from the scorecards. And do not present the existence of the weather
market as an endorsement of trading it; the limits section is what keeps the piece honest.
