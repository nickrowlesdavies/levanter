# Levanter business plan

Honest market intelligence across crypto, foreign exchange and commodities. This
plan covers the business model and how Levanter makes money, with the affiliate
programme and the paid newsletter tier set out in full. It sits alongside the
separate marketing plan, which covers how we grow the audience.

The guiding principle is simple. Revenue should run ahead of engineering. A solo
founder monetises reach and writing first, and only builds paid software once
subscribers are asking for it.

---

## 1. What Levanter is

A market-intelligence brand with two surfaces:

- A free dashboard at levantermarkets.com that reads crypto, foreign exchange and
  commodities on one page: what moved, which market is calm or turbulent, where
  each sits against its own history, plus honest volatility forecasts and a public
  track record.
- A newsletter on Substack: daily notes, and weekly and monthly reviews.

The positioning is the moat. Levanter is the market voice that admits it cannot
predict prices, forecasts only what is forecastable (volatility), and scores every
call in public. That honesty is what people will eventually pay for. It is not the
data, which comes from public sources. It is the judgement on top of it.

---

## 2. Revenue model, in order

Four tiers, sequenced so money arrives before build cost.

1. **Free.** The daily site, the full dashboard basics, and the weekly and monthly
   newsletter. This is reach, funnel and the surface that carries affiliate links.
   It never gets paywalled.
2. **Affiliate.** The first real revenue. Disclosed referrals to tools and venues
   the audience already needs. No engineering, no paywall. Detailed in section 3.
3. **Paid newsletter tier.** A Substack paid subscription for premium commentary
   and alerts. Substack runs the payments, so there is nothing to build. Detailed
   in section 4.
4. **Premium data tools.** Gated dashboard features (custom alerts, cross-device
   watchlists, exports, an API) once paying readers ask for them. This is the only
   tier that needs a backend, and it comes last, funded by the first three.

---

## 3. Affiliate plan

The point is to earn from reach without charging the reader and without bending a
single call. Every link is disclosed, and no partner ever influences the analysis.
Partners are chosen to fit the honesty brand, which rules out high-leverage,
casino-style products we would never recommend in good conscience.

**Partners, in fit order.**

- **Hardware wallets (Ledger, Trezor).** Self-custody and safety, which sits
  naturally with an honest crypto audience. Typically a fixed commission per sale.
  The cleanest first partner because recommending safety is on-brand.
- **Reputable spot exchanges (Kraken, Coinbase).** Regulated, serious venues.
  Usually a revenue share on trading fees or a fixed bounty per funded account.
  Chosen over unregulated exchanges on purpose.
- **Charting and tools (TradingView).** Almost every reader who takes markets
  seriously uses one. A recurring or per-referral commission, and a recommendation
  we would make anyway.
- **Serious brokerage (Interactive Brokers).** For the investor end of the
  audience. An introducing-broker or affiliate arrangement.
- **Books (Amazon Associates).** A short, honest reading list off the About page
  and the deeper pieces. Small per sale, but effortless and evergreen.

**Placement, tastefully.**

- A single "Tools we actually use" page, linked from the footer and About.
- One contextual mention where it actually helps, for example a hardware-wallet
  line in a self-custody explainer, or a charting link beside the cycle gauge.
- A short, standing disclosure on every page that carries a link.
- Never a pop-up, never mid-analysis, never a partner we would not use ourselves.

**Honest economics.** Early affiliate income is small and traffic-bound. A handful
of conversions a month at first. It matters because it costs nothing to run, it
scales with the audience, and it funds the project while the list grows. It is the
breadwinner in year one precisely because it needs no product and no paywall.

---

## 4. Paid newsletter tier (Substack)

The first paid product, chosen because Substack handles payments, billing and the
subscriber list with no engineering. The reader pays for premium judgement and
alerts, not for data.

**Free versus paid.**

- **Free:** the daily note, the full weekly review, the monthly review, and the
  whole live dashboard. The honest core stays open, because the honesty is the
  brand and the funnel.
- **Paid:** a midweek positioning note that reads the models out loud ("what the
  volatility flip means for the week ahead"), the alerts (a volatility-regime
  change, a stablecoin starting to wobble, bitcoin touching its fit floor),
  the full reasoning behind the monthly view, and first access to new tools.

The rule that keeps it honest: paid is extra depth and timeliness, never the
truth held hostage. We do not move a real insight behind the wall to force a
signup.

**Pricing.** Around 10 pounds a month, or roughly 96 pounds a year to reward the
annual commitment. Founding-member pricing at launch to seed the first cohort.
Substack takes 10 percent and card processing takes about 3 percent, so keep
roughly 87 percent of gross.

**The first premium piece.** A midweek "Levanter Signal" note: the week's one
chart that matters, the volatility read across the three markets, and a plain line
on what is and is not knowable from here. It is the taste of the paid tier and the
template for the cadence.

**When to switch it on.** Not day one. Turn it on once the free list is large
enough that a small paid conversion is a real number, roughly in the low thousands
of free subscribers. Before that, everything free, grow the list, run affiliate.

**Illustrative scale, clearly an assumption not a promise.** Newsletters convert
maybe 3 to 6 percent of free readers to paid. At a conservative 4 percent and 10
pounds a month:

- 1,000 free subscribers: about 40 paid, roughly 400 pounds a month gross.
- 5,000 free subscribers: about 200 paid, roughly 2,000 pounds a month gross.
- 20,000 free subscribers: about 800 paid, roughly 8,000 pounds a month gross.

These are planning scenarios to size the opportunity, not forecasts. The honest
read is that the list size is the whole game, which is why the free tier and the
marketing plan matter more than the paywall in year one.

---

## 5. Costs

Deliberately near zero, which is the advantage of a static site and a solo build.

- Hosting: free (GitHub Pages).
- Data: free public sources (CoinGecko, yfinance, blockchain.info).
- Domains: a small annual fee.
- Newsletter: Substack is free until there is paid revenue, then it takes its cut.
- Email and analytics: free tiers.

The only real cost is time. That is the resource to protect, which is why the plan
leans on things that run themselves.

---

## 6. Roadmap and sequencing

- **Now:** free site and newsletter live. Turn on affiliate: the tools page,
  disclosures, and the first two or three partners. Grow the list.
- **Next (list in the low thousands):** launch the Substack paid tier with the
  midweek Signal note and the first alerts. Founding-member pricing.
- **Later (paid readers asking):** build accounts and gate the premium data tools,
  custom alerts and an API. This is the phase-two engineering, funded by the first
  three tiers.

---

## 7. Risks and honesty guardrails

- **Small early numbers.** Expected. Newsletters compound over months. Do not
  judge in week one and do not paywall early to chase revenue.
- **Brand risk from partners.** The single biggest danger is an affiliate deal
  that cheapens the honesty. Mitigation: only partners we use, always disclosed,
  never in the analysis, and never a product we would warn readers away from.
- **Data dependence.** The map runs on free feeds that can rate-limit. Mitigation
  already in place: cached fallbacks so the site never goes blank.
- **Founder time.** The whole model assumes the site runs itself and the writing
  is mostly automated drafting plus a human edit. Keep it that way.

---

## 8. What to measure

- Free subscribers added each week.
- Open rate on the weekly.
- Affiliate clicks and conversions by partner.
- Free-to-paid conversion once the paid tier is live.
- Site traffic and top referrers.

Do more of what adds subscribers. Drop what does not. The one number that governs
everything is the size of the free list, because both affiliate income and paid
conversions scale from it.

---

*The unfair advantage is honesty. Grow the free audience, earn from affiliate while
it grows, add a paid tier for premium judgement and alerts, and build gated tools
only once readers ask. Revenue ahead of engineering, every step.*
